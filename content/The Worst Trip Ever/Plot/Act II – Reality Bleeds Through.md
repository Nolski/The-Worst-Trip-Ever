**Player Knowledge:**

- World no longer fully consistent
- Hallucinations are acknowledged as _something wrong_
- PCs begin questioning morality of their actions

**DM Goals:**

- Increase frequency of reality intrusions
- Escalate consequences of “heroic” actions
- Reveal NPCs with double meanings
- Introduce the idea of **choice**

As the characters complete a few adventures and grow to levels 3–4, they start gaining renown in the fantasy world (perhaps they’re being called the “Chosen Heroes” or similar trope). Traditional fantasy storylines can be used here: e.g. saving a village from “bandits” (who might actually be gangsters in reality), retrieving a “magical artifact” (which could be a stolen tech device or dangerous drug in reality), or even overthrowing an “evil baron” (who in reality is a local authority figure). This middle phase should **lean into familiar fantasy tropes** – it keeps the game fun and recognizable. However, as their deeds become more significant (and more morally gray), the cracks in the illusion grow. Act II is where you should **increase the frequency and clarity of reality glimpses**. The players might start piecing together odd inconsistencies. For example, after toppling the “tyrant” of a city, they glimpse newspaper clippings or a radio broadcast in the real world describing a terrorist act or coup **they** were involved in. The tension rises as the PCs question what is real. By the end of Act II (around level 4), they likely realize that something is very wrong with their world, even if they don’t have the full picture yet.
